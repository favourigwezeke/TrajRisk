\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Mouse integration and the Walesky reference}

TrajRisk combines trajectory inference, learned transition probabilities, and graph neural network propagation to organize hepatocyte states during acetaminophen (APAP)-induced liver injury (\FigRef{1}{A}).

To capture complementary phases of injury and recovery, we assembled four publicly available mouse single-cell RNA-sequencing datasets. The Walesky dataset served as the primary reference cohort, containing hepatocytes sampled across the canonical APAP time course (0~h, 6~h, 24~h, 48~h, and 96~h) (n = 6,582 hepatocytes after timepoint selection and exclusion of partial hepatectomy samples), while three additional datasets provided independent cohorts spanning early injury, recovery, and persistent compensation: the Umbaugh 2~h dataset (n = 21,409), the Ben-Moshe dataset (n = 2,770), and the Umbaugh 48~h/72~h dataset (n = 5,306) (\FigRef{1}{B}). Quality-control characteristics of the three external mouse cohorts are summarized in \FigRefs{S1}{A--S1C}. Together these datasets comprised 36,067 hepatocytes spanning multiple platforms, laboratories, and injury/recovery phases.

Following integration with scVI (\hyperref[sec:methods]{Methods}), all four mouse datasets were represented in a shared 30-dimensional latent space (\FigRef{1}{C}). The low dataset average silhouette width (ASW = 0.039) indicates intermixing. We inferred pseudotime within Walesky to limit the effects of residual cross-platform structure.

Trajectory inference on the Walesky cohort using Monocle 3 \cite{ref047} ordered hepatocytes along a reference spanning baseline, injury, recovery, and persistent compensation (\FigRef{1}{D}). Pseudotime correlated significantly with experimental time (Spearman \ensuremath{\rho} = 0.5051, p < 2.2\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-16}). The 6~h hepatocytes had the highest pseudotime relative to the control root. Hepatocytes at later time points occupied a range of states associated with recovery and persistent compensation (\FigRef{2}{A}). Raw Monocle pseudotime distributions within each sampling timepoint are shown in \FigRef{S2}{A}.

The integrated representation and Walesky-restricted pseudotime provided the features and reference states used to construct directed temporal graphs.

\subsection*{Random Forest classifiers learn directed, treatment-aware transition probabilities}

We trained one Random Forest classifier per temporal scale to predict nearest-neighbor-defined cell-pair transition labels (\hyperref[sec:methods]{Methods}). Five-fold stratified cross-validation yielded AUROC values of 0.9936 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.0002 (Scale 1, 0~h \ensuremath{\rightarrow} 6~h), 0.9871 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.0011 (Scale 2, 6~h \ensuremath{\rightarrow} 24~h), 0.9901 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.0004 (Scale 3, 24~h \ensuremath{\rightarrow} 48~h), and 0.9929 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.0009 (Scale 4, 48~h \ensuremath{\rightarrow} 96~h), with out-of-bag accuracy estimates of 0.961, 0.949, 0.955, and 0.965, respectively (\FigRef{2}{B}; \TableRef{S1}). Euclidean distance in scVI space was the most important feature at every scale, as expected from the nearest-neighbor training labels. The learned transition matrices diverged substantially from a distance-only Gaussian kernel (\FigRef{2}{C}): row-wise Pearson correlations between each learned matrix and its Gaussian baseline were low (Scale 1: 0.246; Scale 2: 0.187; Scale 3: 0.249; Scale 4: 0.403). Cross-validation measured agreement with the assigned neighbor labels.

The resulting matrices remained numerically close to the stochasticity constraint after sparsification (row sums = 1.000, SD < 10\textsuperscript{-6}) and retained high sparsity (99.6-99.9\%). Per-cell transition entropy varied across scales (\FigRef{2}{D}; \TableRef{S1}). Scale 2 had the highest mean entropy (4.88 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.42 nats), and Scale 1 the lowest (3.81 \ensuremath{\pm} 0.26 nats); Scales 3 and 4 had intermediate means of 4.58 and 4.24 nats.

\subsection*{TrajRiskGNN learns biologically coherent trajectory-state representations}

The TrajRiskGNN architecture is summarized in \FigRef{3}{A}. The validation-AUROC-best checkpoint at epoch 144 was used for downstream analyses; training history and validation performance are shown in \FigRefs{3}{B--3D}. The model achieved a validation AUROC of 0.9071 (95\% CI 0.8843--0.9310), AUPRC of 0.7707, and a Spearman correlation of 0.5814 with normalized pseudotime (p = 4.65\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-120}; \TableRef{S2A}). The train-validation Spearman gap was 0.029.

TrajRisk scores followed a pattern consistent with the reference injury trajectory (\FigRef{3}{E}). 0~h root hepatocytes had the lowest mean score (0.008; 0.0\% exceeding a predicted-score threshold of 0.10), while 6~h APAP hepatocytes, sampled during acute injury, had the highest mean score (0.917; 99.3\% exceeding threshold). Mean scores declined at 24~h (0.047, 5.2\% exceeding) and 48~h (0.030, 0.9\%), with a modest rise at 96~h (0.045, 5.6\%) consistent with a persistent compensation subpopulation. Scale-association analysis showed that Scale 1 (Acute Stress) embedding norms were positively associated with the TrajRisk score, whereas Scale 3 (Fate Decision) embedding norms were negatively associated with it (Spearman \ensuremath{\rho} = +0.325 and -0.372, respectively; \FigRef{3}{F}). A PCA of the concatenated 256-dimensional multi-scale representation, colored by sampling timepoint and by predicted TrajRisk score, is shown in \FigRefs{S2}{B and S2C}.

\subsection*{Benchmark comparisons show timepoint-dependent performance}

We benchmarked TrajRisk against four baselines on the fixed 1,318-cell Walesky validation set (\FigRef{4}{A}; \TableRef{S2A}). Expression-only XGBoost \cite{ref048} regression (B1) achieved higher global AUROC than TrajRisk (0.9582 versus 0.9071). This within-reference evaluation was transductive: the TrajRisk graphs included validation-cell pseudotime during construction.

TrajRisk had higher global AUROC than Gaussian scalar propagation (0.856), the development-selected single-scale GNN (0.850), and expression distance from baseline controls (0.800), with one-sided paired-bootstrap p < 0.001 for each comparison (\FigRef{4}{A}). The initial XGBoost seed achieved AUROC 0.875, compared with 0.907 for TrajRisk. The seed is an acute-state classifier trained on 0~h versus 6~h cells, whereas B1 directly regresses continuous pseudotime; these are distinct expression-only comparators.

Reduced-model comparisons showed lower AUROC when learned RF transitions were replaced by Gaussian transitions (decrease of 8.1 percentage points), the seed was removed (7.9 points), one temporal scale was retained (5.7 points), or the complete GNN was replaced by scalar RF propagation (5.4 points) (\FigRefs{4}{A and 4B}; \TableRefs{S2A}{S2B}). Scalar RF propagation removes both nonlinear message aggregation and node-feature integration.

We also evaluated discrimination within recovery timepoints using average precision (AUPRC), with positive labels defined by normalized pseudotime > 0.10 (\FigRef{4}{C}; \TableRef{S2C}). After excluding 6~h cells (n = 1,234; 130 positives), TrajRisk's AUROC was 0.856, compared with 0.796 for the seed and 0.933 for B1. At 24~h (n = 209; 36 positives), TrajRisk had AUPRC 0.498 versus 0.322 for the seed and 0.379 for B1, an 11.9-percentage-point advantage over B1. B1 nevertheless had higher AUROC at each evaluated recovery timepoint. At 48~h, TrajRisk and B1 had AUPRC 0.463 and 0.597, respectively; at 96~h, the corresponding values were 0.581 and 0.692. At 96~h, TrajRisk had a 30.9-point AUPRC advantage over the seed (0.273).

\subsection*{External mouse transfer is cohort-dependent}

We evaluated three external mouse cohorts using frozen Walesky-trained models (\FigRef{5}{A}; \TableRef{S3A}). In Ben-Moshe hepatocytes (n = 2,770), APAP-versus-control AUROC was 0.7197 for Reference-KNN and 0.8069 for \SelfOnly{} TrajRisk, compared with 0.8541 for the seed and 0.8544 for B1. In the Umbaugh 48/72~h cohort (n = 5,306), the corresponding AUROCs were 0.7939, 0.8958, 0.9330, and 0.9423. Both expression-only comparators had higher AUROC in these later cohorts.

In the Umbaugh 2~h cohort (21,409 hepatocytes), \SelfOnly{} TrajRisk had the highest APAP-versus-control AUROC (0.7798), followed by Reference-KNN (0.7480), B1 (0.7313), and the seed (0.7221) (\FigRefs{5}{A and 5B}; \TableRef{S3A}). Both TrajRisk configurations also had higher average precision than the expression-only comparators in this cohort.

In all 5,306 Umbaugh 48/72~h hepatocytes, \SelfOnly{} scores correlated positively with \textit{Cdkn1a} expression (Spearman \ensuremath{\rho} = 0.382; p = 3.84\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-184}; \FigRef{5}{C}; \TableRef{S3B}). Reference-KNN had \ensuremath{\rho} = 0.313, whereas the seed and B1 had stronger correlations (0.418 and 0.404).

\subsection*{TrajRisk transfers the mouse trajectory-state score to human APAP-ALF donors}

Cross-species scVI integration of Walesky mouse hepatocytes and Matchett human hepatocyte nuclei used 13,122 shared mouse--human gene-symbol pairs retained by the uniqueness and expression-axis filters (\FigRef{6}{A}; \TableRef{S4A}). Quality-control characteristics of the 49,984 Matchett human hepatocyte nuclei are summarized in \FigRef{S1}{D}. Both species and condition ASW rounded to 0.0347, consistent with mixing in the joint latent space; the shared cross-species scVI space visualized by species and biological condition is shown in \FigRefs{S3}{A and S3B}. Donor-level \SelfOnly{} TrajRisk scores were higher in APAP-ALF donors (n = 10) than healthy donors (n = 9) (mean 0.2051 vs. 0.1088; Mann-Whitney p = 6.4\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-3}; donor-level AUROC = 0.844; \FigRef{6}{B}; \TableRef{S4B}; \DataRef{S3}). Nucleus-level primary \SelfOnly{} scores across the same cross-species latent coordinates are shown in \FigRef{S3}{C}.

A Reference-KNN sensitivity analysis gave consistent results (p = 8.0\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-3}; AUROC = 0.833; \TableRef{S4B}; \DataRef{S3}). The temporal composition of the 15 nearest Walesky reference neighbors used in this sensitivity analysis is summarized by human condition in \FigRef{S3}{D}. The primary \SelfOnly{} association remained significant after exclusion of low-nucleus-count donors (p = 2.5\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-2}; AUROC = 0.815; \FigRef{6}{C}; \TableRef{S4B}; \DataRef{S4}).

The raw orthologous risk signature, defined by genes positively correlated with mouse pseudotime (\DataRef{S1}), had lower donor means in APAP-ALF than in healthy tissue (0.3585 versus 0.4380) and AUROC 0.056 in its prespecified direction (\TableRef{S4B}). Subtracting the 50-gene recovery signature gave a net-signature AUROC of 0.656, but the directional donor comparison remained non-significant (p = 0.135). Reference-KNN TrajRisk and net-signature donor scores were correlated (Spearman \ensuremath{\rho} = 0.833; p = 9.4\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-6}; \FigRef{6}{D}; \TableRef{S4C}; \DataRef{S3}).

\subsection*{Spatial localization of the orthologous risk signature varies across datasets}

We next evaluated whether the orthologous TrajRisk risk signature localized to spatial regions associated with APAP injury using two independent Visium studies (\FigRef{S4}{}). In the Ben-Moshe spatial dataset, fibrogenic spots marking the pericentral necrotic boundary were significantly enriched for the TrajRisk signature (pooled one-sided Mann--Whitney p < 10\textsuperscript{-300}); enrichment remained significant at 24~h (p = 1.55\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-48}), 48~h (p = 9.37\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-300}), and 72~h (p = 1.19\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-301}), consistent with localization to regions of active injury (\FigRef{S4}{A}; \TableRef{S5A}). These tests pool spots within the profiled tissue.

The risk signature showed no pericentral enrichment in either Matchett spatial dataset. In mouse Visium sections, risk-signature scores were similar between pericentral and periportal spots (one-sided Mann--Whitney p = 0.087; \FigRef{S4}{B}; \TableRef{S5B}), whereas in human sections the pericentral-greater-than-periportal hypothesis was not supported (p = 1.000; \FigRef{S4}{D}; \TableRef{S5C}). Risk-signature scores were negatively correlated with \textit{Cyp2e1/CYP2E1} expression in both mouse (\ensuremath{\rho} = \ensuremath{-}0.427) and human (\ensuremath{\rho} = \ensuremath{-}0.168) tissue (\TableRefs{S5B}{S5C}). In contrast, after excluding \textit{Cyp2e1/CYP2E1} itself from the recovery signature to avoid circularity with zone definition, recovery-signature scores were significantly lower in pericentral than periportal regions in both mouse (p = 1.31\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-57}; \FigRef{S4}{C}; \TableRef{S5B}) and human (p = 2.96\ensuremath{\times}10\textsuperscript{-153}; \FigRef{S4}{E}; \TableRef{S5C}) datasets. APAP-ALF human sections nevertheless showed descriptively higher mean risk-signature scores than healthy sections across pericentral, midlobular, and periportal zones (\FigRef{S4}{F}; \TableRef{S5D}; \DataRef{S5}); no zone-level case-control significance test was performed because spots within a section are not independent biological replicates.

\subsection*{Bottleneck analysis identifies scale-specific programs and a candidate recovery gateway}

Risk flux analysis across 421,477 graph edges (\hyperref[sec:methods]{Methods}) showed that Scale 2 (Peak Injury, 6~h \ensuremath{\rightarrow} 24~h) carried the largest total flux, with 98.5\% of its edges showing negative amplification as TrajRisk scores decreased from the peak. In contrast, Scale 1 (Acute Stress, 0~h \ensuremath{\rightarrow} 6~h) edges showed uniformly positive amplification. The heavy-tailed risk-flux distributions across all four scales are shown in \FigRef{7}{A} (\TableRef{S6A}). Scale 1 ablation produced the largest total change in TrajRisk scores, showing the greatest sensitivity of the frozen model to removal of that scale (\FigRef{7}{D}; \TableRef{S6B}).

Scale 3 (Fate Decision, 24~h \ensuremath{\rightarrow} 48~h) showed the highest flux concentration, with 9.8\% of total scale flux carried by the top 1\% of edges (\TableRef{S6A}). The pseudotime distribution of top-flux source cells was bimodal at Scale 3, spanning both recovery-proximal and persistent-compensation 24~h states (\FigRef{7}{B}). The 43 unique 24~h source cells feeding these high-flux edges were enriched for elevated scores and pseudotime within the 24~h population: all 43 were in the top TrajRisk-score quartile among 24~h cells, and 76.7\% were high-pseudotime. By contrast, only 18.9\% of the 380 unique 48~h target cells were high-pseudotime, below the 25.1\% expected background proportion (\FigRef{7}{C}; \TableRefs{S6C}{S6D}). The source-cell enrichment was robust to the bottleneck definition: across top 0.5\%, 1\%, 2\%, 5\%, and 10\% Scale 3 flux thresholds, the fraction of high-pseudotime source cells remained above the 25.0\% background among all 24~h cells (\FigRef{S5}{A}; \TableRef{S6E}). These edges define a candidate resolution gateway linking compensation-associated 24~h states to recovery-proximal 48~h states. Because flux includes the source score, the selection favors high-scoring sources. In a complementary perturbation analysis, mean absolute 48~h score changes remained small across source-cell pseudotime quartiles (0.0015--0.0018), whereas the Q4 high-pseudotime group produced the largest maximum single-cell change (0.0979); all 96~h mean and maximum effects were zero (\FigRef{S5}{B}; \TableRef{S6F}).

Pathway analysis showed scale-specific programs: Scale 1 bottleneck targets were enriched for oxidative phosphorylation; Scale 2 targets showed lower expression of CYP-associated metabolic programs; Scale 3 targets showed downregulation of ribosome biogenesis, mitochondrial translation, and immediate-early stress-response programs; and Scale 4 targets showed enrichment of periportal-associated programs (\FigRef{7}{F}). Scale 3 bottleneck edges also showed significantly larger embedding shifts than background edges (p < 10\textsuperscript{-172}; \FigRef{7}{E}; \TableRef{S6G}), consistent with Scale 3 being a representational as well as a flux bottleneck.

We separately assessed human differential-expression overlap with mouse bottleneck-associated programs. Among 2,549 tested mouse-gene mappings, 1,036 (40.6\%) met the nominal human differential-expression criterion in APAP-ALF versus healthy donors (p \ensuremath{\leq} 0.05; \DataRef{S2}). These records correspond to 1,023 significant human genes among 2,534 distinct human genes tested (40.4\%). The corresponding distinct-human-gene overlap was 46.6\%, 46.6\%, 37.8\%, and 41.9\% for Scales 1--4, respectively (\FigRef{6}{E}). The overlap counts genes at nominal significance without requiring agreement in fold-change direction.
