\newcommand{\manuscriptabstract}{%
Single-cell profiles capture how hepatocytes respond to acetaminophen, but it remains difficult to connect the states observed during injury and recovery. We developed TrajRisk to learn directed transition graphs between successive sampling times and integrate gene expression across these graphs with a multiscale graph neural network. The resulting score estimates a hepatocyte's position along a mouse injury--recovery reference. At 24~h, TrajRisk had higher average precision for identifying cells with elevated pseudotime than an expression-only model. Performance varied across external mouse cohorts. Scores from a mouse-trained model distinguished human donors with acetaminophen-associated acute liver failure from healthy donors. The associated gene signature was enriched along mouse injury boundaries. Graph flux identified a candidate 24--48~h recovery gateway connecting states associated with persistent compensation to states closer to recovery. TrajRisk prioritizes transitions and gene programs for experimental studies of liver repair.%
}
